I bought a Keychron R3.

The first unit, however, did not work properly over USB. Windows showed it as an “Unknown USB Device (Device Descriptor Request Failed),” so I contacted the retailer and had it replaced as an initial defect.

I previously wrote about the troubleshooting process. This time, I want to focus on how the replacement unit actually feels in daily use.

My conclusion is simple: ==the setup is a little confusing at first, but as a keyboard for writing in Japanese, I like it a lot.==

:::conclusion
The keys initially felt slightly small, but I adapted almost immediately. The Japanese layout feels natural, with the keys exactly where I expect them to be. The typing sound is fairly loud, but I enjoy it when writing, and the weight of the keyboard gives it excellent stability on a desk.
:::

## The Japanese layout feels very natural

The first thing I liked was how little adjustment the Japanese layout required.

There are almost no awkward points in the arrangement. ==The keys I want to press are where my fingers expect them to be.==

I have used ThinkPad keyboards for a long time. Even a small difference in layout can make your fingers hesitate for a fraction of a second, even when you consciously know where a key is. With the R3, I have almost none of that hesitation.

It does not feel like a keyboard that becomes comfortable only after you retrain yourself. It feels natural from the beginning.

If I had to name one small issue, I occasionally hit Insert by mistake. I do not think this is unique to the R3, though. It is a fairly common kind of accidental keypress with layouts of this type.

## The keys felt a little small, but I adapted immediately

When I first touched the keyboard, the keys felt slightly smaller than I expected.

That concern disappeared very quickly. It did not take days of adjustment; I simply stopped noticing it after a short period of use.

So I would separate two things: the key size felt slightly unfamiliar at first, while the layout itself did not feel unfamiliar at all.

## It is clacky and loud. I like that

The R3 is not a quiet keyboard.

It makes a noticeable clacking sound, and I can imagine it being difficult to use in a very quiet office or around coworkers who are sensitive to keyboard noise.

But I knew that before buying it. More importantly, after using it, I realized that I actually like the sound.

When I am writing, every keystroke gives a clear sense of feedback. From a purely acoustic standpoint, the noise is an obvious drawback. For long-form writing, however, that sound gives me a satisfying sense that I am actively producing text.

I also like ThinkPad keyboards, but the R3 feels satisfying in a very different way.

## It feels relatively easy on the fingers during long writing sessions

I have not used it for months yet, but so far it feels relatively comfortable for long-form typing.

Even after writing continuously for 30 minutes or an hour, I do not feel unusually fatigued in my fingers.

Typing feel is subjective, of course, but for me this is not just a keyboard that sounds enjoyable. It is also practical for sustained writing.

## It is heavy, and that is both a strength and a weakness

The R3 is fairly heavy.

If portability matters, that is clearly a disadvantage. This is not the kind of keyboard I would casually throw into a bag every day.

On a desk, though, the weight becomes an advantage. It stays planted and does not shift around easily while typing.

This is a straightforward trade-off.

If you want something lightweight, the mass is a weakness. If you want a stable, desk-bound writing tool, I actually like the weight.

## Bluetooth supports three devices, but the “1, 2, 3” system confused me at first

The setup process is one of the less intuitive parts of the experience.

The R3 can store three Bluetooth devices and switch between them. That is a genuinely useful feature.

At first, however, I did not understand what the Bluetooth “1, 2, 3” options meant. Once I realized that they were simply three device slots, it became obvious, but it was not immediately intuitive to me.

:::note
For people who regularly switch between multiple devices, having three Bluetooth slots is a strong feature. I probably will not use all three very often, but it is nice to have the option available.
:::

As for reconnecting after sleep, I have not noticed any particular problem. The fact that I cannot immediately remember a frustrating reconnection issue is probably a good sign: it has not been intrusive enough to become part of the experience.

## It is highly customizable, but I am satisfied with the defaults

The R3 allows key remapping and customization.

That flexibility is often one of the major attractions of enthusiast keyboards, but I have been happy using the default configuration.

Function keys and Japanese input work normally for me. I do not use function keys heavily in the first place, but nothing about ordinary Japanese text entry feels problematic.

Customization is useful to have, but I appreciate that the keyboard does not require customization to become comfortable.

## I do not need the RGB lighting, but it can be turned off

The lighting is quite noticeable.

I am not someone who wants a keyboard glowing on the desk, so it initially felt unnecessary.

Fortunately, it can simply be turned off.

I lose nothing by using it with the lighting disabled, so I do not consider this a meaningful drawback. It is just a feature I personally do not need.

## The initial defect was frustrating, but I separate that from the product itself

Receiving a defective first unit was obviously disappointing.

Buying a new keyboard and immediately seeing “Device Descriptor Request Failed” in Windows is not a good first experience.

Still, the retailer replaced it, and the replacement unit has worked normally. For that reason, I think it is fair to separate the fact that I received a defective unit from my evaluation of the R3 as a keyboard.

My impression improved significantly once I was actually able to use it.

## Conclusion: slightly awkward to get started, very satisfying once I begin writing

If I had to summarize my current impression of the Keychron R3 in one sentence, it would be this: ==getting started was a little troublesome, but once I begin writing, it feels extremely satisfying.==

I had an initial hardware defect. The three Bluetooth slots were not immediately obvious to me. The keys felt slightly small for a moment.

But once I started typing, those concerns became much less important.

The Japanese layout feels natural. My fingers do not search for keys. The clacking sound is loud, but it gives writing a pleasant rhythm. The body is heavy, but stable. And long typing sessions feel relatively comfortable.

I do not like the R3 because it has a long feature list. ==I like it because, as a tool for writing a lot of Japanese text, it simply feels good to use.==

That is currently my favorite thing about it.
