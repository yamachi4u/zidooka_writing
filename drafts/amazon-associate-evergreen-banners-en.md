When you write affiliate articles, it is natural to wonder whether Amazon Associates offers banners you can use all year, not just during Prime Day or another sale.

The short answer: check the link creation tools inside Associates Central first. Depending on the current interface and your locale, you may see product links, text links, image links, campaign banners, or category-oriented materials.

But a generic “shop at Amazon” banner is rarely the best first choice. Readers already know Amazon. What they need is help choosing the right product in the context of the article.

:::conclusion
If you want evergreen Amazon Associates assets, start with the official tools in Associates Central. In practice, contextual product links and image links usually work better than generic Amazon banners.
:::

## Where to Look First

Amazon Associates is built around special tracking links. You recommend products or Amazon services through those links, and qualifying actions can generate commission income.

So the first place to look is not a logo image from the web. It is the official link creation area.

Check for options such as:

- product links
- text links
- image or text-and-image links
- seasonal campaign banners
- category or promotion links

Menu names change over time. If you cannot find the exact item, search inside Associates Central for terms like “banner,” “link creation,” or “product links.”

## Do Not Make Your Own Amazon Logo Banner

This is the part to be careful with.

Do not grab an Amazon logo, add your own copy, and publish it as a custom affiliate banner. Amazon Associates is a program with specific rules, and you should stick to official tools, approved assets, and compliant link formats.

:::warning
Avoid making your own “shop at Amazon” banner with Amazon branding. Use assets and links provided through Associates Central, or use normal affiliate links generated by the official tools.
:::

## Generic Banners Usually Underperform

The problem is simple.

People do not read your monitor guide, Kindle comparison, or gadget review because they need to discover Amazon as a store. They read it because they need help deciding.

For a monitor article, the useful questions are things like:

- Which screen size should I choose?
- Is 60Hz enough for office work?
- Do I need USB-C power delivery?
- Which budget trade-offs are acceptable?
- Which model fits this use case?

A generic Amazon banner does not answer any of those questions.

A product link placed at the right moment does. So does a category link when the reader still wants to compare options.

## Better Link Patterns

If I were setting up an article, I would use these patterns before looking for a generic banner.

## Product Review or Comparison Articles

Use individual product links when the article talks about specific products.

Good placements include:

- the product you reviewed
- each item in a comparison table
- accessories that solve a concrete problem

The link should appear when the reader has enough context to act.

## Category or Buying Guide Articles

If the article does not recommend one exact product, use search or category links.

Examples:

- a link to search for “27-inch 4K monitor”
- a link to compare Kindle devices
- a link to browse USB-C hubs

This works well when readers still want to choose for themselves.

## Sale Articles

For Prime Day, Black Friday, or other campaign articles, campaign banners may be available.

Those assets are often temporary. If you use them, plan the cleanup too. An expired sale banner makes an article look neglected fast.

## How to Decide

The easiest way to choose is to ask what the reader is trying to do.

:::note
If the reader is deciding what to buy, use product links. If the reader is comparing a broader category, use category or search links. A banner that only says “go to Amazon” should usually be lower priority.
:::

In affiliate articles, the goal is not merely to send someone to Amazon. The better goal is to reduce the work of choosing. Links feel more natural when they continue the article instead of interrupting it.

## Summary

Amazon Associates may provide reusable materials through Associates Central, so that is the right place to check first.

Still, generic banners are not magic. Contextual product links, image links, and category links usually fit the reader’s intent better.

Use official tools. Avoid custom Amazon-logo banners. Place links where they answer the reader’s next question.

That is usually a stronger setup than hunting for a broad “shop at Amazon” banner.
